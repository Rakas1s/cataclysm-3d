# Divergences from CDDA

Every deliberate rule change from vanilla CDDA goes here, with the reason.

| System | CDDA behavior | Ours | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Looking | No facing | Free mouse-look, costs no moves | First-person camera |
| Mapgen (M1, M5) | Places items, monsters, nested chunks, loot, vehicles | Terrain, furniture, monsters (M4) and, since M5, items: the `items` and `item` symbol tables (palettes and `mapping` too) and `place_items`, `place_loot`, `place_item`, with CDDA's rules (`chance` is a percent per `repeat`; groups roll whole). Items roll from their own random stream, so loot doesn't change a seed's terrain. Not placed: nested chunks, vehicles, `sealed_item` (seeds in planters), and loot's `ammo` / `magazine` / `variant`; `place_items` with an x/y range picks its tile per axis without CDDA's 20 retries for a free tile | Nested mapgen comes later |
| Mapgen (M1) | `predecessor_mapgen` generates the underlying terrain first | `t_null` keeps `fill_ter` (or dirt) | Not implemented yet |
| Smashing (M1, M5) | Bash strength from Str + wielded weapon; noise alerts monsters | Str plus your weapon's bash (since M5), at least 10 as before, against CDDA's `str_min`–`str_max` roll; no noise yet | No sound until M6 |
| Locked doors (M1) | Open from inside; lockpicking and keys from outside | Open from inside (standing on a roofed tile); smash from outside | Lockpicking needs items and skills |
| Mapgen (M2) | Parameters with `"scope": "overmap_special"` are rolled once per building, so every floor shares them | Rolled per z-level, so floors of one house can differ (e.g. paint) | Each level is a separate mapgen run for now |
| Overmap (M2) | Overmap generation grows cities along roads from `city_building`s in any rotation, with CDDA's road mapgen | A fixed six-house town: two rows of single-tile buildings facing a straight, hand-laid road (sidewalk, pavement, dashed line) | Full overmap generation is post-MVP; multi-tile buildings and east/west rotations come later |
| Z-levels (M2) | Stepping onto open air (`NO_FLOOR`) drops you to the level below, with fall damage | Open air is impassable ("You'd fall") | No falling or damage yet |
| Stairs (M2) | You take stairs with `<` / `>` while standing on them | Walking onto stairs or a ladder takes it right away; arriving on the other end doesn't, so you can step off. `<` / `>` still work | Fewer keypresses in first person |
| Stairs (M2) | Going up or down lands on matching stairs found across the map, or prompts when there are none | The nearest matching stairs within 12 tiles, else straight up/down if walkable; stairs and ladders both cost 100 moves | Close enough for CDDA houses, whose stairs line up exactly |
| Climbing (M2) | Climbing a downspout or gutter drop is an examine/climb action with a chance to fail | `<` / `>` next to any `CLIMBABLE` terrain always succeeds, costs 200 moves, and lands on the nearest walkable tile above/below it | No skills or failure rolls yet |
| Tilesets (M3) | One tileset at a time; an id without a sprite falls back to its `looks_like` chain within that tileset | Several tilesets in priority order (`config/tilesets.json`). Each id in the `looks_like` chain is tried in UndeadPeople, then Ultica, before moving down the chain, so Ultica's sprite for an id beats UndeadPeople's sprite for its `looks_like` | Lets UndeadPeople be the look while Ultica fills its gaps |
| Tilesets (M3) | Multitiles connect through `connect_groups` / `connects_to` | Connects to neighbors with the same id; walls connect to any `WALL` | Connect groups aren't imported yet |
| Tilesets (M3) | Seasonal sprites follow the calendar | Always spring, CDDA's starting season | The calendar exists now (M3), but seasonal art is left for later |
| Look (M3) | Top-down sprites | Floors, roofs and ground show their sprite (fg over bg) as their top surface. Walls repeat their sprite every meter; for walls only UndeadPeople's art is used (Ultica's are drawn with bevelled 3/4 tops that don't tile as a face), so walls with only Ultica art are plain voxels in that sprite's mean color. Doors are painted from their sprite. Furniture is extruded from its sprite (front-facing, top-down or upright silhouette, by id keyword) | 3D presentation; see `FurnitureModels.cs` |
| Furniture facing (M3) | Sprites always face the viewer; most furniture has no rotation | Seats face an adjacent table or desk; other single-tile furniture turns its back to an adjacent wall. Connected pieces (tables, counters, beds, sofas) keep map orientation | In first person, a fridge facing the wall would look wrong |
| Sun and moon (M3) | Sun position from the calendar, latitude and a time zone; natural light from the sun's altitude, weather and moon phase | Same 91-day seasons and latitude 42.36°N, but solar noon is 12:00 and spring day 1 is exactly the equinox. Light by sun altitude: night light until -18°, up to 3.7 at civil dawn (-6°), 40 at sunrise, 100 at 60° and above. Night light is 1 + 2.25 per lit quarter of the moon; the calendar starts on a waxing half moon. No weather or clouds | Close to CDDA's curve without its astronomy and weather code |
| Daylight indoors (M3) | Sunlight shines in through windows and openings as directional light | Every roofed see-through tile next to the open sky (windows, doorways, porch edges) is a point source as bright as the sky outside, falling off with distance | Simpler; rooms are lit near windows and dark deep inside, as in CDDA |
| Light falloff (M3) | Light attenuates through each tile by its transparency | Inverse square from one tile out (luminance / (d + 1)²), cut off where it drops below 3.5 (CDDA's `LIGHT_RANGE`); no attenuation through air, smoke or fields | No fields yet; gives the same range as CDDA |
| Sight (M3) | You see lit tiles in line of sight; your own and adjacent tiles even in the dark | Same, with the threshold 3.5 (`LIGHT_AMBIENT_LOW`) and no night vision or eye mutations. A wall shows the light on the side facing you. Other z-levels: you see down through open air and stairwells; levels above aren't fogged on screen (and the sim counts them as unseen) | No 3D FOV yet; in first person you see upper storeys' outsides anyway, and the cutaway hides them indoors |
| Flashlight (M3) | An item (`flashlight_on`, `LIGHT_300`) you activate, which drains batteries | Still toggled with L for 100 moves, with no flashlight item and no batteries, even though items exist since M5. Same brightness, omnidirectional | Tool use (activating items, charges) comes later |
| Waiting (M3) | `5`/`.` waits a turn; `\|` opens a wait menu; waits are interrupted by danger | `5` waits a turn, `\` 30 minutes, `\|` until the next sunrise or sunset. Since M4 a wait stops when a hostile monster you couldn't see comes into view, or when you get hurt. `.` stays bound to `>` (down) from M2 | No wait menu yet |
| Monster spawns (M4) | The overmap places city monster groups with density; mapgen `place_monsters` rolls pack sizes from `density`; `place_monster` places packs | Mapgen `place_monster` (`chance` percent per `repeat`) and `place_monsters` (one-in-`chance`, one monster per `repeat`, `density` ignored) are honored, plus 6 zombies from `GROUP_ZOMBIE` on random outdoor tiles. Nothing spawns within 12 tiles of where you start. Group rolls skip entries that aren't hostile zombies (civilians, wildlife) and generated pseudo monsters (dormant zombies), and those with a later `starts` | No overmap yet; M4 is about zombies |
| Monster types (M4) | Many fields: special attacks (grab, bite, scratch), upgrades, revival, death drops, morale, fear, sounds, size | HP, speed, aggression, melee skill and dice, `melee_damage`, `attack_cost`, dodge, bash/cut/stab armor, `vision_day`/`vision_night`, `bash_skill` (default dice × sides, as CDDA does) and the `SEES`, `BASHES`, `GROUP_BASH`, `CAN_OPEN_DOORS` flags. No special attacks, upgrades, revival, death drops or morale | Basic M4 combat first |
| Monster AI (M4) | Monsters track the player by sight, sound and scent; idle ones mostly stand or follow sounds; they use stairs | Sight only: a monster with `SEES` notices you when you're within its sight range (CDDA's `sight_range` by the light on your tile, so a flashlight gives you away) and in line of sight, found by shadowcasting from its tile. It walks (A*, 8 directions) to where it last saw you, then wanders. Idle monsters set off for a random tile within 6 about one turn in five. Monsters never change z-level | No sound or scent until M6; z-level pathing later |
| Doors (M4) | Monsters bash obstacles in their way with `bash_skill`; `GROUP_BASH` monsters add their strength together | Same rule as the player's smash (fails when a roll between `str_min` and `str_max` beats the strength). A hunting monster plans its path through doors and windows its own `bash_skill` reaches, priced by the expected number of smashes; adjacent `GROUP_BASH` monsters add theirs when smashing. A lone zombie (6) breaks windows (3–6) but never a door (8–80) | Keeps zombies from beating on doors they can't break alone |
| Melee (M4, M5) | To-hit and damage from stats, skills, weapon, techniques, encumbrance; crits; monster grabs and bites | Hit rolls are a normal roll around accuracy × 5 (spread 25) against dodge × 5, as in CDDA. Your accuracy is Dex / 4 plus your weapon's `to_hit` (no skills); a monster's is its `melee_skill`. Since M5 damage and speed follow CDDA 0.I's `roll_melee_damage_internal` and `attack_speed` for a survivor with no skills: bash is weapon bash + U(Str/2, Str), capped near 2 × Str, rolled down to 40–100 % (Str/20 floor), × 0.8; cut × 0.8, stab × 0.66; an attack costs 2 × ⌊`attack_time` / 2⌋ − Dex / 2 moves (60 bare-handed, 106 with a bat), plus CDDA's stumble penalty on a miss. No crits, techniques, stamina, size or limb modifiers, and encumbrance doesn't affect melee yet. A monster's blow is its dice in bash plus its `melee_damage` | No skills or martial arts yet |
| Body parts (M4) | 12 body parts (hands, feet, eyes, mouth separate); HP scales with Str and traits; broken limbs hamper you; bleeding, pain | 6 main parts from `body_parts.json` with their `base_hp` (60); minor parts add their `hit_size` to their main part. Blows land by `hit_size`. You die when the head or torso hits 0. A limb at 0 takes no more damage and nothing else happens | Simplest version that keeps CDDA's feel |
| Death (M4) | Monsters leave a corpse item with butchery and revival; you die and the game ends | A dead monster leaves a corpse marker drawn from its corpse sprite (not an item you can pick up). When you die, Enter starts a new run in a new town | Butchery comes later |
| Monster art (M4) | Top-down sprites | Monsters are upright billboards of their sprite (one random variant per monster) that turn to face you, 1/16 m per pixel. Corpses lie flat. Only monsters on tiles you can see are drawn | Clearest in first person |
| Starting gear (M4, M5) | You pick a profession and start with its items, which protect, encumber and warm you | You start with the Unemployed profession's whole kit (since M5): its clothes worn, innermost layer first, and the rest (pocket knife, water, phone, wallet, matches) in your pockets; groups in the kit are rolled. Male by default, `--female` for the female sprite and clothes. Your hair is a random CDDA hair style trait (from `appearance_hair_styles.json`, not bald) in a natural color (no dyed blue, green, pink or purple), rolled from the town seed, where CDDA has you pick it. All portrait layers come from the tileset that has the body sprite, so clothes line up; what you wield is drawn on top (`overlay_wielded_*`) | No character creation yet |
| Items (M5) | Item types carry dozens of subtypes and fields (tools, guns, books, spoilage, variants, faults, qualities, materials' fire and acid data) | Loaded: name, description, weight, volume, `longest_side`, flags, materials, `melee_damage` (or the legacy `bashing` / `cutting`), `to_hit` (number or 0.I's grip/length/surface/balance object, -2 when missing), `armor`, `warmth`, `material_thickness`, `max_worn`, CONTAINER `pocket_data`, `comestible_type`, `calories`, `quench`, `charges`, `container` and `phase`. Variants (t-shirt prints), damage levels, spoilage and vitamins are ignored | Only what M5 uses; the rest as later milestones need it |
| Item groups (M5) | `collection` / `distribution` / old-style groups with `prob`, `count`, `charges`, `container-item`, `ammo`, `magazine`, `damage`, `variant`, `on_overflow`, `ITEM_SPAWNRATE` | Collections, distributions (old-style groups are distributions, inline ones collections), `items` / `entries` / `groups` in every form, `prob`, `count`, `charges`, `container-item` on entries and groups. Food comes in its default container; liquids fill theirs. A `count` of an item counted by charges folds into one stack. `ammo`, `magazine`, `damage`, `variant`, `sealed` and `on_overflow` are ignored, and the spawn rate is CDDA's default 1 | Covers what houses spawn |
| Pockets (M5) | Every pocket of every item is its own container with volume, weight, length and item-count limits; nested containers; holsters; pocket-specific move costs and rules (rigid, watertight, sealed, ripoff) | Each worn item's CONTAINER pockets are pooled into one space: an item fits if it fits the biggest pocket on its own (volume, length) and the pool has room for its volume and weight; liquids need a watertight pocket. No base volume, as in CDDA, so a naked survivor carries nothing. A wielded bag holds what's in it but doesn't count as pockets, and you can't put things into a container that's in your pocket or on the ground | Simpler bookkeeping; close for clothes and bags |
| Inventory reach (M5) | `g` picks up from your tile; the advanced inventory reaches the tiles around you | You can pick up, wield, wear and eat items on your tile and the eight around it (picking up from the next tile costs CDDA's 100 moves more), and drop onto any of them | The drag-and-drop screen shows the tiles around you |
| Wearing (M5) | `can_wear` checks mutations, power armor, splints, `RESTRICT_HANDS`, one-per-layer and rigid conflicts per sub-body-part and side, and `max_worn` | `max_worn` (default 2), `ONE_PER_LAYER`, and the rigid rule, which keeps you to one pair of shoes: a portion is rigid if any of its materials isn't `soft` (`HARD` / `SOFT` flags override) and it's on the skintight, normal or outer layer. Checked per body part (left and right feet separately) rather than per sub-part (sole, heel), so a rigid shoe upper and a rigid sole conflict | No mutations, power armor or sub-body-parts yet |
| Armor and encumbrance (M5) | Per-material, per-sub-part protection with `covered_by_mat`; layering penalties per sub-part and out-of-order layers; encumbrance slows actions and melee and hampers dodging | An item's protection is its first `armor` entry's materials (resist × thickness × cover), else its materials sharing `material_thickness`; stab is cut × 0.8 when a material doesn't say. A blow to an arm, leg or the head lands on the hand, foot, eyes or mouth by hit size, then each item covering that part stops it with its `coverage` chance, outermost first. Encumbrance (with soft pockets filling towards `max_encumbrance`) and CDDA's layering penalty (each item on a layer but the bulkiest adds its encumbrance again, clamped 2–10) are shown and raise handling costs through hand encumbrance, but don't slow movement, melee or dodging yet. Warmth is shown per part; there's no temperature | No body temperature or limb scores yet |
| Move costs (M5) | Item handling through `item_location::obtain_cost`, `item_handling_cost`, `item_wear_cost`, `item_store_cost`, with grabs, handcuffs, skills and bulk pick-up | The same formulas: handling is a base plus 1 per 20 ml (at most 200) plus your freer hand's encumbrance; off the map the base is 80 plus 100 a tile away; out of a pocket it's the pocket's `moves`; taking off is handling at 50 plus 250; wearing is handling × the layer factor × the average encumbrance / 10; setting things down costs `cost_to_move_item`. Several items from one pile cost only their bulk after the first. Stowing into a pocket costs the store cost with no skill | Faithful where it matters |
| Carry weight (M5) | Over your weight capacity (13 kg + 4 kg per Str) you lose speed; past 4 × capacity you can't pick things up | Same capacity and speed penalty (25 per capacity's worth over); no hard limit on picking things up, and pockets limit you anyway | — |
| Hunger (M5) | Stomach and guts with volume, water, calories and vitamins, digested in steps; hunger effects from stomach fullness, time since eating and fat BMI; the metabolism follows your activity level; starvation by BMI | Stomach (2.5 L, starting with 475 ml and 800 kcal) and guts (300 kcal) as CDDA: every half hour the stomach passes a sixth of its capacity and its calories on, the guts feed your fat store, which burns 1738 kcal a day (a new survivor's BMR at rest, awake or asleep: activity levels aren't tracked). The labels are 0.I's (Engorged, Full, Satisfied, Peckish, Hungry, Near starving, Famished), including its quirk that "Starving!" never shows. No vitamins, spoilage, morale from food, vomiting, or speed loss from a low BMI until the fat store runs low; you die when it's empty | No activity tracking or health yet |
| Thirst (M5) | +1 per 5 minutes (half asleep); water reaches you through the stomach; speed penalty; dehydration damage and death at 1200 | Same rates, labels (from thirst less a tenth of the water in your stomach), speed penalty and death; no dehydration messages every 30 minutes | — |
| Eating and drinking (M5) | A consume activity of a second per 5 ml of food (15 ml of drink), refusals when overfull, allergies, cooking, heating, spoilage, medicine | Same times as moves, one charge at a time, from your pockets or the ground; the empty container stays. You can't eat or drink what would overfill your stomach. Medicine isn't usable yet, and no allergies, cooking, heating or spoilage | — |
| Sleep (M5) | `$` starts trying to sleep; the fall-asleep roll (comfort, sleepiness, stimulants, rng(-8, 8)) every 30 minutes; sleep for up to 10 hours, recovering about 2 sleepiness per 5 minutes; light, temperature and noise wake you; sleep deprivation; microsleeps | Same roll (with CDDA's comfort: furniture's `comfort`, else the terrain's less its move cost, +4 for a `SLEEP_AID` item on your tile), checked when you lie down and every half hour, giving up after four tries; refused outright when the roll can't succeed. Recovery 2 per 5 minutes from the start, waking a few minutes after sleepiness reaches 0, or after 10 hours. Woken by getting hurt, a hostile monster within 6 tiles that sees you, or bad thirst; not by light or noise. At 1000 sleepiness you pass out, as in CDDA; no microsleeps, no sleep deprivation | No sound until M6; no temperature |
| Item art (M5) | Items lie on the map as their top-down sprite | Piles are upright billboards of their sprites (half a tile across, up to three per pile, scattered), standing on the floor or on tables, counters and beds; items inside container furniture (fridges, dressers) aren't drawn. Shown where you see or remember the tile | Readable in first person |
| Inventory screen (M5) | Key-driven menus: `i` inventory, `w` wield, `W` wear, `T` take off, `d` drop, `g` pick up, `E` eat, and the advanced inventory | One drag-and-drop modal over the 3D view: a paper doll with a slot per body part and your hands, your pockets by container, and the nine tiles around you. Drag between them to wear, wield, take off, pick up and drop; places that would take the item turn green, the rest red, with the reason. CDDA's keys work on the item under the mouse (`w`, `W`, `T`, `d`, `g`, `E`). Outside it, `g` picks up everything on your tile, `E` / `W` / `T` open it asking what to eat / wear / take off, and `$` sleeps; plain `w` and `d` step (WASD), so wielding and dropping by key happen in the screen | First person needs the keys for walking; dragging is the user's request |
